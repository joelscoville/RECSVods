# Operator Brief: Concise Service Outlines

The current chapter migration produces too many top-level chapters and displays too much descriptive prose. This brief supersedes the chapter-count, boundary, summary and presentation guidance in `2026-09-26-chapter-search.md` where they conflict. Apply it to the current migration before its final acceptance and to the Milestone 5 weekly curator procedure. The existing source, human-review, media, privacy and publication rules remain in force. Use **Title Case for every public chapter and subsection title**; keep ordinary sentence casing for descriptive prose.

## What a visitor needs

A visitor should be able to navigate the whole service, understand the sermon's main argument, and find relevant parts through search. The primary outline should make worship, scripture, sermon and closing material easy to find without making every activity a separate peer chapter. The sermon portion should read like an outline of its argument.

Show exactly one concise **sermon description beneath the video** (and in the corresponding service-level context): natural prose that conveys the sermon's central direction and main supporting ideas through its narrative flow, attributed to the speaker where appropriate. Let readers understand the argument without explicit labels such as "Thesis:" or "Points:", a numbered outline, or a fixed introduction–problem–solution template. Follow the shape and emphasis of the sermon itself. It describes the sermon within the recording, not every event in the service and not a claim that the whole video contains only a sermon. Do not assemble it by concatenating chapter summaries. Do not show descriptions beneath individual chapters or subsections.

## Choosing visible chapters

First identify the sermon thesis and the major points that support or develop it. Then mark a boundary only when the speaker moves to a materially different part of that argument: for example, passage and background, the problem being addressed, the passage's response, application, and conclusion. A new example, scripture citation, named topic, song, or speaker turn does not by itself require a new chapter. Adjacent material that serves the same point belongs together. Do not split a coherent argument to satisfy a target duration or count, and do not force an outline onto a sermon whose structure is uncertain.

Most full services should have a **small number of sermon chapters**, often around three to six, but this is a judgment guide, not a quota. A single coherent sermon chapter is valid; a longer or unusually structured sermon may need more. Titles should name the point being made, not just list a topic. Boundaries need enough evidence to make playback useful; fewer, sound boundaries are preferable to many speculative ones.

Worship songs, routine prayers, call to worship, readings, offertory, benediction and announcements should not each become a top-level chapter merely because they are distinct events. Use neutral, content-based service groups such as **Opening**, **Worship and Scripture**, and **Closing Remarks and Offertory** when the recording supports them. These are peer navigation entries alongside the sermon movements; do not call them "Before the Sermon" or "After the Sermon", or style them as less important. Adjust group order or labels to the actual recording rather than implying a fixed liturgy. A distinctive choir item or substantial independent address may merit its own peer chapter or an optional subsection on its own merits, but it should not be forced into the sermon's argument. An incidental announcement, such as a wedding notice, needs no searchable subsection. Preserve accurate source timing and any existing evidence internally; reducing visible chapters must not erase the recording or invent silence.

If precise search benefits from smaller units, retain evidence-backed **subsections or search cues under a parent chapter**. They may carry timestamps, scripture references and keywords, but they must not multiply the primary chapter list. A search hit can deep-link to a subsection timestamp while showing its parent chapter and service context. Sermon subsections should represent meaningful support for its thesis, not every change of subject or passing mention. All subsection titles use Title Case.

## Writing and search metadata

For each visible chapter, prefer a clear **Title Case** title and a compact set of distinctive, source-supported phrases, topics and scripture references. Keep descriptions, synopses and supporting detail **internal only** where needed for search and human review. They must not render in chapter rows, subsection rows, watch-page chapter navigation, service outlines or search-result cards. Public entries should show title, time and only sparse useful metadata. Search results can explain a match with its parent title, service context, scripture or a short match reason, without exposing the internal description.

Do not fill missing evidence with a polished description. Attribute claims to the speaker and retain uncertainty. Search metadata should be strong enough to retrieve the sermon and let an editor write a faithful description, but it must not imply that an incidental mention is a principal chapter or a sermon thesis.

## Source and transcription scope

Verify the recording identity, timing and sermon boundaries. Use authorized captions or transcription to cover the sermon and any adjacent material needed to establish its context and chapter boundaries. Subsection or transcribe additional material only when it contributes to the overall message or is independently important enough to warrant a secondary marker. Routine service material need not receive exhaustive transcription just to populate chapters. Keep new raw captions/ASR private under the existing evidence rules; do not commit or publish verbatim transcripts. Preserve all previously committed passage/transcript material unchanged in its internal archive.
